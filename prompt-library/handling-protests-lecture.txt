OPENING SCENARIO
You Are the ASP
You are ASP of a subdivision.
400 employees are protesting in front of the State Secretariat, demanding implementation of Pay Commission recommendations.
Employees voted en masse for the ruling party on a promise: implementation within 6 months of coming to power.
2 years have passed. The promise is unmet.
An indefinite sit-in has been running peacefully for 28 days.
Protestors now threaten self-immolation if the demand isn't met within 2 days.
What is your response?

SAME PROTEST, FIVE DIFFERENT RESPONSES
Know Your Tolerance Level
Running example: Government Employees Association protest for pay revision — 500–800 members, outside the Collectorate, known repeat organizer, peaceful history.
1
Zero Tolerance
No gathering permitted.
Preventive detention of organizers before assembly.
2
Gathering Allowed, Not Disruption
Assembly allowed at a designated point.
Traffic and access cannot be disrupted.
3
Graduated Ceiling for Disruption
Disruption tolerated up to a fixed limit (time / duration).
Then forcible action is pre-authorized.
4
Leadership-Targeted
Isolate/arrest key leaders with the calculation that followers disperse without direction.
5
Life-Risk Override
Non-negotiable : a genuine risk to life triggers intervention regardless of the set ceiling.

TOLERANCE LEVEL
Tolerance Can Shift Mid-Protest
DAY 1
Level 3 — graduated tolerance running as calibrated: 30-min traffic window, sit-in tolerated up to 3 days.
Trigger:
An opposition political party publicly announces support and plans to join the protest site.
A service issue risks becoming a political flashpoint the moment a party attaches itself to it.
DAY 2
Tolerance hardens: Level 3 effectively becomes Level 1–2.
Sit-in ceiling compresses — may need to end same-day
Traffic-block window may shrink to zero
Police posture visibly stiffens
Tolerance must be actively RE-CONFIRMED with the competent authority whenever the situation materially changes — never assumed to hold same from the morning briefing.

WHAT YOU MUST KNOW BEFORE PLANNING TACTICS
PRE – EVENT INTELLIGENCE
Numbers & Composition
Expected headcount, organizer identity, first-time group vs. repeat player with a known pattern.
Stated vs. Actual Intent
What organizers say publicly vs. what intelligence suggests. The gap between the two is where officers are caught unaware.
Escalation Indicators
Static gathering or foot rally? Effigy burning? Gherao or forcible-entry intent? Hunger strike — symbolic or relay, day-only or overnight?
Violence Risk Markers
Stone-pelting history, known troublemakers present, self-immolation threats, night-continuation plans.

--------------------------------------------------
SLIDE 6
--------------------------------------------------
PRE-EVENT INTELLIGENCE
Good Intelligence Narrows Surprise — It Doesn't Eliminate It
USUALLY KNOWABLE
Expected headcount
Organizer identity & affiliations
Stated intent (public statements)
Group's protest history
HARD TO PREDICT IN ADVANCE
Whether stone-pelting actually breaks out
Whether a self-immolation threat is genuine or rhetorical
Whether a peaceful gathering turns violent
A third party (e.g. a political group) attaching to the cause
An SP who over-trusts the briefing and under-prepares for the unbriefed scenario is more dangerous than one who assumes some uncertainty going in.

Synthesis - One Decision Chain

→
INTELLIGENCE
What's actually happening
→
TACTICAL PLAN
Force, staffing, standby
DAY 1
Level 3 tolerance + low-risk intelligence → moderate barricading, modeate sized force, medical & night-shift standby prepared. No forward-deployed lathi/tear gas.
DAY 2
Intelligence shift (opposition support) → tolerance re-confirmed and hardens → posture stiffens, barricades reposition. Plan is REVISED, not just continued.

FORCE CONTINUUM
Force Escalation Ladder
Always start at the lowest rung necessary. Document why you moved to the next one.
1
Presence
Visible deployment, no active intervention.
2
Verbal warning / dialogue
Direct communication with organizers on compliance.
3
Formal dispersal order
Issued under legal authority when informal compliance fails.
4
Arrest of leaders
Remove leadership under law; crowd typically disperses on its own once leaders are gone.
5
Mild physical force
Minimum force to clear a specific obstruction, if the crowd still hasn't dispersed.
6
Baton / tear gas / water cannon
Only when lower rungs have genuinely failed.
7
Firing
Absolute last resort. The most heavily scrutinized in any inquiry.

SLIDE 9
--------------------------------------------------
FORCE CONTINUUM
Two Different Questions
TOLERANCE ASKS:
“How much am I permitted to let this go on?”
Set by government / administration. Governs deployment posture and thresholds for action.
PROPORTIONALITY ASKS:
“Given I'm acting, how much force does this moment justify?”
Never changes because tolerance hardened. Hardened tolerance changes how soon you move through the lower rungs — not which rungs you may skip.
Confusing the two — “government wants a hard line, therefore I can use more force than warranted” — is exactly the reasoning that fails at inquiry.

SLIDE 10

WHO TALKS TO WHOM, AND WHY IT MATTERS
Command and Communication
1
Organizer Liaison
A designated senior officer maintains direct, continuous contact with protest leadership — before and throughout.
2
Clear Chain of Command
One clear chain of command for the deployed force. Never ambiguous, never diffused across officers.
3
Upward Reporting
Continuous flow to the SP / competent authority — especially anything that should trigger a tolerance re-check.
4
Media Spokesperson
A designated voice. In its absence, rumours and video clips outrun the official version within minutes.

SECTION 9 · CLOSING THE LOOP
A Range of Responses — Each Correct Under Different Conditions
A. Wait and watch
No concession; prepare for forcible removal if the threat of self immolation materializes.
B. Facilitate talks
Push the underlying policy decision urgently to top political leadership.
C. Divide and Rule
Isolate/remove the persons threatening; negotiate main demand with moderate group on a separate track.
D. Disperse Before Day 30
Forcibly clear the sit-in to remove the opportunity for self-immolation.
Situation 1:
Demand for pay revision is likely to be met by Government.
The demands are seen to be reasonable by public at large.

SECTION 9 · CLOSING THE LOOP
The Correct Response?
None of the four responses can be fully chosen without knowing two things :
What is government's tolerance level for this specific issue?
Is the demand itself something that can realistically be met?
